# Recommended Product Model: **Platform Fee Audit Membership**

Do not sell the GPT as “access to an AI tool.” That is easy to copy and difficult to retain.

Sell a recurring operational outcome:

> **Know where your ecommerce money went, which fees are bleeding margin, which SKUs are worth scaling, and when payout timing may create a cash crunch.**

The GPT becomes the analysis engine. Wix becomes the paid member portal and community. Notion becomes the operating workspace. Your higher tiers add human review, implementation, and recurring finance-operations support.

## The Product Ladder

| Tier | Core promise | Customer does | You provide | Suggested price |
|---|---|---|---|---:|
| **DIY — Fee Audit Lab** | Run your own platform-fee and margin audits | Uploads, reconciles, reviews findings | GPT, templates, training, group support | **$49–$99/month** |
| **DWY — Margin Recovery Desk** | Complete the audit with expert guidance | Supplies files and implements decisions | Guided audits, office hours, workspace, reviews | **$299–$750/month** |
| **DFY — Ecommerce Finance Ops Desk** | Outsource recurring platform reconciliation and cash visibility | Approves classifications and actions | Monthly reconciliation, reporting, recommendations | **$1,500–$4,000/month** |

These should be separate levels of responsibility—not merely the same content with more Zoom calls.

A self-service membership for ecommerce sellers who know they are paying fees but cannot clearly answer:

- Did every payout reach the bank?
- What percentage of revenue disappeared into platform costs?
- Which SKUs actually produce contribution profit?
- How much cash is trapped in reserves, holds, refunds, or timing gaps?
- Are platform deductions increasing faster than sales?

## DWY Monthly Workflow

### Week 1 — Collect

- Client submits platform reports.
- Client submits payout exports.
- Client submits bank deposits.
- Client updates COGS and advertising data.
- System identifies missing documents.

### Week 2 — Reconcile

- Run GPT-assisted normalization.
- Match settlements to payouts.
- Match payouts to deposits.
- classify fees, reserves, refunds, and financing deductions.
- generate exception report.

### Week 3 — Diagnose

- Analyze fee variance.
- update SKU margin scores.
- review reserve exposure.
- calculate payout timing.
- estimate working-capital pressure.

### Week 4 — Act

- Review findings in group or private session.
- assign corrective actions.
- prepare disputes requiring human approval.
- improve the next reporting cycle.
- identify funding-readiness gaps where relevant.

## Service Boundaries

To keep DWY productized:

- Limit supported platforms by plan.
- Limit file volume or reporting periods.
- Use one standardized monthly reporting cadence.
- Provide one revision cycle.
- Require customer approval for assumptions.
- Do not include bookkeeping cleanup.
- Do not file disputes automatically.
- Do not contact platforms without explicit approval.
- Do not provide tax or accounting treatment.
- Route custom data engineering to DFY or a separate project.

## DWY Events

Use Wix Events for:

- Monthly payout close clinic
- Amazon settlement teardown

- Reserve and hold office hours
- Quarterly funding-readiness review

Wix can connect events to recurring membership plans and offer member-specific access or discounts. ([Wix Support](https://support.wix.com/en/article/wix-events-creating-a-membership-plan?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Do not position this as “we run your GPT.”

Position it as:

> **A productized finance-operations desk for ecommerce sellers who need recurring payout reconciliation, platform-cost visibility, SKU margin reporting, and cash-timing intelligence without hiring a full internal finance team.**

### Suggested Names

- Ecommerce Finance Ops Desk
- Platform Margin Control Desk
- Payout Reconciliation Desk
- Ecommerce Cash Visibility Desk
- Marketplace Margin Desk
- Platform Fee Recovery Desk

My recommendation: **Ecommerce Finance Ops Desk** as the umbrella, with **Platform Margin Control** as the core monthly service.

## Suggested Pricing

### Essentials — $1,500/month

Best for one primary platform.

- One platform
- One payment processor
- One bank account
- Monthly reconciliation
- Fee variance report
- Reserve tracker
- Executive summary
- One review call

- SKU margin scorecard
- advertising-cost allocation
- reserve and hold review
- cash-timing analysis
- dispute-opportunity report

### Control Desk — $4,000+/month

- monthly close package

- SKU profitability review
- reserve aging
- payout-delay monitoring

- funding-readiness package

### Setup Includes

- export verification
- column mapping
- sign-convention verification
- opening reserve balances
- baseline fee rates
- initial payout reconciliation
- initial SKU margin model

## DFY Deliverables

Every month, deliver a standardized package:

1. Executive finance-operations summary
2. Platform settlement reconciliation
3. Payout-to-bank reconciliation
4. Unmatched transaction report
5. Fee variance dashboard
6. Reserve and hold report
7. SKU margin scorecard
8. Cash-timing diagnosis
9. Dispute-opportunity register
10. Working-capital gap summary
11. Funding-readiness update
12. Next-action plan

The customer should receive the same recognizable operating package every month. Standardization is what makes the service scalable and defensible.

## What DFY Should Not Become

Avoid becoming:

- A generic bookkeeping agency
- A fractional CFO service
- An Amazon reimbursement contingency firm
- An ad-spend management agency
- A custom dashboard development shop
- A lender promising approvals
- A manual spreadsheet cleanup service with no recurring system

Your wedge is narrower:

> **We reconcile the money between ecommerce platforms and the bank, identify margin leakage, and turn the findings into cash-flow and funding-readiness decisions.**

# 5. Best Customer Segmentation

Route prospects by complexity rather than company size alone.

## DIY

Best for:

- Solo ecommerce operators
- Early Shopify stores
- Creators selling digital or physical products
- Sellers below roughly $50,000 in monthly sales
- Operators comfortable with spreadsheets and ChatGPT
- Businesses that need structure but cannot justify monthly service fees

## DWY

Best for:

- Sellers with $25,000–$250,000 in monthly revenue
- One to three platforms
- Confusing fees or payout mismatches
- Growing advertising spend
- Incomplete SKU economics
- Recurring cash-timing problems
- Operators who need accountability and interpretation

## DFY

Best for:

- Multi-channel sellers
- Meaningful platform reserves
- High refund or return volume
- Large catalogues
- Complex advertising attribution
- Multiple currencies
- financing remittances
- weekly cash pressure
- founders preparing for working capital, inventory financing, or growth capital

These are operating hypotheses for packaging—not underwriting thresholds or guarantees.

# 7. Recommended Launch Sequence

## Phase 1 — Sell the Audit Before Building the Membership

Launch:

### **90-Day Platform Margin and Payout Audit**

Charge **$750–$1,500** for the first five to ten clients.

Use those engagements to identify:

- Files customers actually have
- common missing fields
- platform-specific confusion
- time required per audit
- highest-value findings
- objections
- recurring tasks
- support load
- which reports customers understand

This prevents you from building a membership around hypothetical needs.

## Phase 2 — Convert the Repeated Process Into DWY

After several audits:

- Standardize onboarding.
- create the Notion workspace.
- document the monthly cycle.
- establish scope limits.
- create office hours.
- price ongoing guided support.

## Phase 3 — Extract the DIY Membership

Turn repeated explanations into:

- lessons
- checklists
- templates
- prompts
- recorded audits
- FAQs
- event curriculum

The DIY tier should be extracted from proven client work—not created first as a giant course.

## Phase 4 — Introduce DFY Selectively

Offer DFY only to clients who:

- submit files on time
- have enough complexity to justify the service
- will maintain clean source access
- understand the service boundaries
- can approve classifications promptly
- value monthly reporting

# Strategic Recommendation

Lead with the **DWY Margin Recovery Desk**.

DIY is scalable but requires audience volume. DFY generates revenue but risks becoming labor-heavy before your process is standardized. DWY is the strongest first model because it:

- monetizes your judgment
- uses the GPT without selling mere GPT access
- generates recurring customer data
- creates reusable templates and training
- reveals which work should be automated
- creates natural upgrades to DFY
- produces material for the DIY membership

Build the ladder in this order:

> **Paid audit → DWY membership → DFY managed desk → DIY membership → partner licensing**

That sequence turns the GPT into a revenue engine, operating system, lead qualifier, and delivery layer—rather than another useful tool sitting in a directory.
